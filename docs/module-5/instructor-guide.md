# Module 5 Instructor Guide

## Content retained in Module 5

Keep availability, representativeness, base-rate neglect, small-sample bias, the gambler's fallacy, hot-hand beliefs, and overconfidence together. They form a coherent module about probability judgment, pattern detection, and confidence.

Retain the three-part confidence distinction: overestimation, overplacement, and overprecision. Use overprecision for calibration exercises, overplacement for relative-ranking discussions, and overestimation for forecasts of personal performance.

## Content moved from Module 5

Move the longer material on emotional distress, self-concept threat, self-regulation failure, belonging, and decision fatigue to a later module on intertemporal choice, self-control, and emotion. Module 5 should retain only a short diagnostic bridge so students recognize that not every judgment error is explained by a probability heuristic.

Move affective forecasting and adaptation after major life events back to Module 3, where predicted, experienced, and remembered utility are already compared.

Move cognitive dissonance to the module on social preferences, identity, or post-choice rationalization. It is conceptually distinct from Bayesian and sampling errors.

## Hot-hand framing

Do not teach the hot-hand question as settled by one study. The supplied presentations contain older null findings and later evidence involving contests, calibration, shot selection, and negative recency. Use the topic to teach identification: independence, selection of harder attempts, learning or warm-up, sample construction, and the difference between belief and performance.

## Important wording revision

Replace “causes of irrationality” with “decision states and environmental pressures.” The revised language is more consistent with the course's Adaptive Species framework and avoids treating emotional or social conditions as character defects.
