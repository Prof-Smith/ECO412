## Purpose of the field study

Your objective is not to determine whether people are rational or irrational. You will identify how a choice is presented, document what is directly observable, develop a possible behavioral explanation, consider an alternative explanation, and state what your observation cannot establish.

<div class="caw-sequence">
  <article><b>1</b><h3>Select</h3><p>Choose one bounded decision environment.</p></article>
  <article><b>2</b><h3>Observe</h3><p>Record only what is visibly present.</p></article>
  <article><b>3</b><h3>Interpret</h3><p>Connect the feature to a Module 1 concept.</p></article>
  <article><b>4</b><h3>Challenge</h3><p>Develop an alternative explanation and limitation.</p></article>

## Step 1: Select a decision environment

Choose one setting in which a person must notice, compare, select, continue, decline, purchase, register, dispose of something, or take no action.

<div class="caw-examples">
  <article><small>CAMPUS OR WORKPLACE</small><p>Registration form, appointment screen, student-service page, sign-up process, directional sign, queue, service counter, or dining display.</p></article>
  <article><small>CONSUMER SETTING</small><p>Menu, subscription page, pricing display, checkout screen, product comparison, return process, or loyalty-program invitation.</p></article>
  <article><small>PUBLIC ENVIRONMENT</small><p>Recycling station, donation prompt, transportation screen, public-service form, health display, or parking-payment interface.</p></article>
</div>

A suitable setting has a recognizable decision moment, at least two possible actions including inaction when relevant, one or more visible design features, and an observation method that does not collect private or identifiable information.

!!! warning "Privacy and ethics boundary"
    Observe the environment, not identifiable people. Do not collect names, student identification numbers, private records, protected-characteristic information, identifiable photographs, faces, covert audio, or covert video. Do not change the environment or conduct an experiment on other people.

---

## Step 2: Define the decision

Identify the setting, the general decision-maker role, the decision moment, the available actions, and what happens if no action is taken.

> **Example:** An online university appointment page asks a student to select an in-person or virtual appointment before viewing available times. The student may also leave the page.

---

## Step 3: Identify one primary choice-architecture feature

<div class="caw-features">
  <article><small>DEFAULT</small><h3>What happens without an active change?</h3><p>Preselection, automatic enrollment, renewal, or recommended settings.</p></article>
  <article><small>ORDER AND PLACEMENT</small><h3>What appears first or most prominently?</h3><p>Position, sequence, ranking, center placement, or a difficult-to-find alternative.</p></article>
  <article><small>SALIENCE</small><h3>What attracts attention?</h3><p>Color, size, contrast, bold wording, warnings, badges, or highlighted information.</p></article>
  <article><small>FRICTION</small><h3>Which action requires more effort?</h3><p>Additional screens, fields, waiting, searching, cancellation steps, or repeated confirmation.</p></article>
  <article><small>SIMPLIFICATION</small><h3>What reduces complexity?</h3><p>Short lists, grouped information, summaries, recommended paths, or progressive disclosure.</p></article>
  <article><small>SOCIAL INFORMATION</small><h3>What suggests what others do?</h3><p>Popularity labels, ratings, participation percentages, testimonials, or normative messages.</p></article>
  <article><small>REFERENCE POINT</small><h3>What becomes the comparison point?</h3><p>Original prices, suggested contributions, bundles, premium options, or side-by-side comparisons.</p></article>
</div>

---

## Step 4: Separate observation from interpretation

<div class="caw-evidence">
  <article><small>OBSERVATION</small><h3>What is directly visible?</h3><p>“The recommended option appears first, has a colored border, and is preselected.”</p></article>
  <article><small>INTERPRETATION</small><h3>What may the feature do?</h3><p>“The placement and preselection may reduce search effort and make the recommended option easier to complete.”</p></article>
  <article><small>NOT ESTABLISHED</small><h3>What can you not claim?</h3><p>“This observation does not prove that the design changed behavior or reveal the designer's intention.”</p></article>
</div>

**Prefer:** “The preselected option appears to reduce the effort required to complete that path.”

**Avoid:** “The default makes people select the option.”
