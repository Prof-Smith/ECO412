# Module 7 Lens: Other People Are the Environment

<div class="asl-hero asl-m7"><small>THE ADAPTIVE SPECIES • MODULE 7</small><h1>We adapt to agents who are adapting to us.</h1><p>In strategic environments, beliefs about other players become part of the environment, and individually sensible responses can generate collectively weak outcomes.</p></div>

## Principle

Game theory maps players, actions, information, beliefs, and payoffs. Material incentives identify equilibria and strategic tensions.

## Dissent

Trust, reciprocity, inequity aversion, guilt, identity, reputation, norms, and punishment can alter behavior. A payoff matrix may omit what players believe they owe one another.

## Reversal

A low-trust or noncooperative action may be locally adaptive when exploitation is likely, information is weak, or future interaction is uncertain. The deeper failure may be institutional rather than personal: the game selects behavior that harms the wider system.

## Local fitness and system fitness

A behavior can survive because it protects one player while degrading the collective outcome. Adaptive institutional design changes the payoff, information, timing, commitment, or norm structure so that individually viable behavior supports system performance.

## Five adaptive questions

1. What game are the players actually playing?
2. What does each player believe about the others?
3. Which behavior is locally rewarded?
4. Which norm or focal point coordinates action?
5. How could the game change without relying on moral exhortation?

## Applied reflection

Map one weak equilibrium in a campus, market, or team setting. Explain why the current behavior survives, then propose a redesign and one unintended consequence.

<div class="asl-takeaway"><strong>Adaptive takeaway</strong><span>A bad collective outcome does not require bad people. It may require good local adaptation to a poorly designed game.</span></div>
