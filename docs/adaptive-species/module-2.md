# Module 2 Lens: Ideas Under Selection

<div class="asl-hero asl-m2"><small>THE ADAPTIVE SPECIES • MODULE 2</small><h1>Let the explanation fail before reality does.</h1><p>Experiments are environments in which ideas compete, encounter evidence, and either survive, change, or disappear.</p></div>

## Principle

A credible study specifies a question, treatment, comparison, outcome, assignment method, and inferential rule before the result is known.

## Dissent

A persuasive finding can be produced by selection, confounding, weak measurement, researcher discretion, sampling variation, or context. A compelling story is not a research design.

## Reversal

A failed replication does not always mean that the original mechanism never existed. The effect may depend on population, stakes, timing, information, incentives, or implementation. Replication maps the environment in which an idea survives.

## Evolution at the speed of thought

Humans can generate possible explanations, subject them to simulated selection, and revise them before high-cost consequences arrive. Preregistration, pilots, premortems, and replication turn scientific humility into a working practice.

## Five adaptive questions

1. What belief is being tested?
2. What result would weaken that belief?
3. Which features of the setting are essential to the mechanism?
4. What source of noise could imitate the proposed effect?
5. What should be preserved, revised, or rejected after the evidence arrives?

## Applied reflection

Write a falsification condition for one behavioral hypothesis. Then describe one null result that should change the theory and one null result that might instead reveal a boundary condition.

<div class="asl-takeaway"><strong>Adaptive takeaway</strong><span>A good experiment does more than produce a result. It creates a disciplined pathway for belief revision.</span></div>
