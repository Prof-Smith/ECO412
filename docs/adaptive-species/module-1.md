## Apply the five-part lens

<div class="m1one-lens">
<article><b>01</b><small>PRINCIPLE</small><h3>Benchmark</h3><p>What does the relevant SEM assumption predict?</p><div>If choice is context independent, then…</div></article>
<article><b>02</b><small>DISSENT</small><h3>Challenge</h3><p>What does the lab result or field observation challenge?</p><div>Under this condition, the observed response…</div></article>
<article><b>03</b><small>REVERSAL</small><h3>Possible function</h3><p>What search, information, coordination, or commitment problem may be reduced?</p><div>This shortcut or design may be useful when…</div></article>
<article><b>04</b><small>MISMATCH</small><h3>Failure of fit</h3><p>When could the same response create hidden costs or reduce autonomy?</p><div>The fit may break when…</div></article>
<article><b>05</b><small>REVISION</small><h3>Answerable claim</h3><p>What evidence would weaken the preferred account or favor the alternative?</p><div>I would revise this interpretation if…</div></article>
</div>

## Worked example

<div class="m1one-worked"><section><strong>Principle</strong><p>A context-independent decision-maker evaluates all options rather than treating preselection as informative.</p></section><section><strong>Dissent</strong><p>A preselected option is easier to complete and may receive more attention.</p></section><section><strong>Reversal</strong><p>A reliable default can reduce search and administrative effort.</p></section><section><strong>Mismatch</strong><p>The default can become costly when user circumstances differ or alternatives are obscured.</p></section><section><strong>Revision</strong><p>The account weakens if choice remains unchanged after preselection is removed or workflow fully explains the ordering.</p></section></div>

!!! note "Interpretive boundary"
    Adaptive does not mean optimal, desirable, conscious, universal, or currently beneficial.
