# Module 1 Lens: From Error to Environmental Fit

<div class="asl-hero asl-m1"><small>THE ADAPTIVE SPECIES • MODULE 1</small><h1>What problem is the behavior solving?</h1><p>The rational-actor benchmark is useful because it makes departures visible. The adaptive lens asks what those departures reveal about constraints, objectives, and environmental fit.</p></div>

## Principle

The Standard Economic Model provides a disciplined benchmark: preferences are coherent, money is fungible, beliefs update consistently, and choices reflect stable objectives.

## Dissent

Actual behavior may display preference reversals, social giving, mental accounts, base-rate neglect, and context effects. These patterns challenge the model as a complete description of human judgment.

## Reversal

A departure is not automatically a defect. Giving may reflect social utility. A mental account may protect a long-term goal. A shortcut may save search time. Context may reveal information that the formal model omitted. Adaptive analysis does not excuse poor judgment; it identifies what the behavior fits.

## Five adaptive questions

1. What constraint made full optimization costly or impossible?
2. What information was available at the moment of choice?
3. Which objective did the observed behavior appear to serve?
4. What environmental feature made the shortcut useful?
5. What evidence would show that the shortcut had become mismatched?

## Applied reflection

Choose one Module 1 result. Write two explanations: first, an error account; second, an adaptive-fit account. Identify one observation that could distinguish them.

<div class="asl-takeaway"><strong>Adaptive takeaway</strong><span>Do not replace the rational benchmark. Use it, then ask whether the model omitted a constraint, value, or environmental cue.</span></div>
