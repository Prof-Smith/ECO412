# Module 6 Lens: Accounts as Commitment Architecture

<div class="asl-hero asl-m6"><small>THE ADAPTIVE SPECIES • MODULE 6</small><h1>Boundaries can protect goals and hide opportunities.</h1><p>Mental accounts simplify economic life, preserve commitments, and make distant goals concrete. The same boundaries can create rigidity.</p></div>

## Principle

In the benchmark model, money is fungible, sunk costs do not determine future choices, and ownership alone should not change underlying use value.

## Dissent

People label money, segregate gains, integrate losses, respond to reference prices, value transactions separately from products, and often demand more to surrender an owned object than they would pay to acquire it.

## Reversal

Mental accounts can protect savings, control spending, reduce cognitive load, and support self-control. Ownership can preserve identity or continuity. Payment coupling can improve cost awareness. The adaptive problem is deciding when the boundary supports discipline and when it blocks a superior reallocation.

## Account ecology

```text
Label → Boundary → Commitment → Behavior
                  ↘ Rigidity or hidden opportunity cost
```

## Five adaptive questions

1. What goal does the account protect?
2. Which reference point defines gain or loss?
3. What information does payment timing make visible or invisible?
4. What future opportunity is excluded by the account boundary?
5. What rule would allow disciplined exceptions?

## Applied reflection

Choose one budget category, subscription, investment label, or ownership decision. Explain both the commitment benefit and the flexibility cost.

<div class="asl-takeaway"><strong>Adaptive takeaway</strong><span>A useful mental account should be strong enough to guide behavior and permeable enough to respond to better evidence.</span></div>
