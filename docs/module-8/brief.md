# Behavioral Insights Capstone Brief

<div class="cap-brief-banner"><strong>The capstone is a decision document, not a long catalog of biases.</strong><span>A strong submission makes one bounded claim, supports it with appropriate evidence, proposes a proportionate intervention, and explains how the recommendation could be tested.</span></div>

## The project in one sentence

> For **[specific decision-maker]**, redesign **[one decision environment]** to improve **[one observable behavior]**, because **[behavioral mechanism]**, while testing against **[strongest alternative explanation]**.

## Suitable project environments

Choose an accessible setting where a decision can be observed or represented without collecting private information. Suitable environments may include a campus process, student service, financial-choice interface, subscription or pricing display, budgeting tool, public message, team workflow, consumer setting, or digital form.

Avoid broad topics such as “improve financial literacy,” “reduce bias,” or “make students healthier.” Narrow the project to a decision, moment, and measure.

## Required project package

### 1. Executive brief

Write 1,500–2,000 words for a real decision-maker. Use this structure:

1. Executive recommendation
2. Decision environment and target behavior
3. Behavioral diagnosis
4. Evidence and limitations
5. Intervention prototype
6. Evaluation plan
7. Implementation, ethics, and unintended consequences
8. Bounded conclusion

### 2. Behavioral diagnosis map

Show the relationship among:

```text
Environment → Cue or friction → Belief or motive → Behavior → Consequence
```

Add the strongest alternative explanation and one observation that would help distinguish the explanations.

### 3. Original evidence exhibit

Use one de-identified source:

- Environmental observation
- Existing nonprivate data
- Structured self-observation
- Class research records
- Instructor-supplied data

The exhibit must state the sample or observation count, measure, comparison, and limitation. Descriptive evidence should not be presented as causal evidence.

### 4. Intervention prototype

Create a decision-ready representation, such as revised wording, disclosure, sequence, default, reminder, feedback display, commitment device, private-choice process, account structure, or coordination mechanism.

### 5. Evaluation plan

Specify:

- Treatment
- Comparison
- Primary outcome
- Assignment or comparison method
- Planned sample and stopping rule
- Exclusion rule, if any
- Ethical safeguard
- Unintended consequence to monitor
- Result that would weaken or reverse the recommendation

### 6. Professional presentation

Present the problem, evidence, recommendation, prototype, evaluation plan, and limitation. The presentation should be understandable to a decision-maker who has not taken behavioral economics.

## Course integration requirement

Use at least three mechanisms from different portions of the course. One mechanism should be designated primary. The other mechanisms should clarify the context, provide an alternative explanation, or identify an unintended consequence.

## Ethical boundary

Do not collect names, private records, protected-characteristic data, photographs of identifiable people, covert audio, or covert video. Do not deploy an intervention involving other people without instructor approval. Observation and prototype design do not establish causal impact.
