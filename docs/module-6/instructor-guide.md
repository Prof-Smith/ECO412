# Module 6 Instructor Guide

## Content retained in Module 6

Keep the mental-accounting material on reference points, integration and segregation, transaction utility, activity-specific accounts, choice bracketing, paper losses, sunk costs, payment decoupling, flat-rate bias, and budgeting together. The presentation explicitly connects prospect-theory value coding with mental accounting and provides applications involving consumers, investors, corporations, and payment timing.

Bring forward the ownership material previously moved from the earlier presentation set: willingness to pay versus willingness to accept, endowment effect, loss aversion after ownership, IKEA effect, trial ownership, and money-back guarantees. These concepts become more coherent when students can compare product value, deal value, payment history, and ownership.

## Content moved from Module 6

Move the social-norms presentation to a later module on social preferences, reciprocity, incentives, and institutions. The social-versus-market distinction, daycare fine, unpaid requests, money priming, and corporate “neighbor” language are not mental accounting mechanisms. They concern norm activation and incentive crowding.

## Content additions recommended

Add a short treatment of choice bracketing and payment depreciation because both organize several examples already in the presentation. Add a distinction between a sunk cost and a rational replacement cost. Continue to require an alternative explanation before labeling behavior a mental-accounting effect.

## Applied-finance opportunity

The investor example is especially strong for the course. Use a de-identified portfolio scenario to distinguish economic loss, tax realization, mental booking, concentration risk, and reallocation. Avoid asking students to disclose personal holdings.
