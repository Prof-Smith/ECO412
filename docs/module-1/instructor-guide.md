# Instructor Guide

## Positioning

The lab treats the Standard Economic Model as a benchmark rather than a straw person. Students should ask whether an observed departure reflects error, context-sensitive preferences, social preferences, transaction costs, limited attention, or a model that omits a valued outcome.

## Suggested facilitation

### Before the lab

1. Introduce normative versus descriptive models.
2. Explain that experiments isolate mechanisms rather than reproduce all features of life.
3. Ask students to commit to first responses.

### During the lab

Pause after Stations 2, 5, and 7. Invite predictions before students reveal the results. Avoid describing a behavioral answer as inferior.

### Debrief prompts

- When does simplification make the SEM useful?
- When does a systematic departure matter for prediction?
- Can a preference be stable if it depends on context?
- Is generosity inconsistent with rationality, or only with narrow self-interest?
- When does choice architecture improve stewardship, and when does it become manipulation?

## Class-level comparison without a server

The site intentionally stores responses locally. For a class visualization, students can submit exported JSON files through the LMS. Combine de-identified files outside the public site. Do not publish individual profiles.

## Accessibility and privacy

Provide a non-interactive alternative if needed. Do not interpret the generated scores as validated psychological measures. The tasks are designed for instruction and discussion.
